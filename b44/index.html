Built from the ground up.

Eight stages go into a concrete slab, and most of what decides how long it lasts is finished before the surface is. Asphalt jobs start the same way: forms, grade and a compacted base.

  1. 01

    Forms set to grade

    Wood forms are staked and leveled to the finished height, with a slight slope so water runs away from the house.

  2. 02

    Compacted road base

    Gravel road base goes down in layers, and each layer is compacted. This is what keeps a slab from sinking and cracking.

  3. 03

    Steel reinforcement

    Rebar is tied in a grid and set on chairs, so it sits inside the slab instead of on the ground beneath it.

  4. 04

    The pour

    Air-entrained concrete goes straight from the truck into the forms and is spread before it starts to set.

  5. 05

    Screed

    A straight edge pulled across the forms strikes the surface flat and at the right height.

  6. 06

    Bull float

    Floating closes the surface and settles the stone just below it, for a dense top that stands up to salt.

  7. 07

    Edges and control joints

    Rounded edges resist chipping. Control joints decide where the slab is allowed to crack: in a straight line, out of sight.

  8. 08

    Broom finish

    A last pass with a broom leaves a fine texture for grip on wet or icy days. Then the slab cures.

Common questions

How long does a new driveway last in Utah?

With a properly compacted base and regular maintenance, an asphalt driveway typically lasts 15–20+ years and a concrete driveway 25–30+ years. Drainage and base prep make the biggest difference.

When is the best time of year to pave?

Asphalt and concrete are best installed when temperatures are consistently above about 50°F — generally spring through fall in Utah County. Repairs and estimates can be scheduled year-round.

How soon can I drive on my new driveway?

Asphalt is usually ready for cars in 2–3 days (longer in hot weather). Concrete needs about 7 days before vehicle traffic; foot traffic is fine after 24–48 hours.

How often should asphalt be sealcoated?

Every 2–3 years. Wait at least 6–12 months after a new install so the asphalt can cure, then sealcoat to protect it from UV, water and oil.

Can you pave over my existing driveway?

Sometimes. If the base is solid and damage is only on the surface, an overlay can save money. If there's heaving, sinking or widespread cracking, a full tear-out and replacement is the right call — we'll tell you honestly which one you need.
