## Design Specificity Verdict

**Unusually well-grounded above the fold, generic below it — the page gets less like a paving company the further you scroll.**

The top ~900px could not be lifted onto another business. `.section-cut` is real product thinking: three stacked layers rendered as actual materials in CSS (noise-on-#242424 asphalt; three offset radial-gradient dot patterns that genuinely read as compacted gravel; a graded brown subgrade), each carrying a real depth and a number. That figure *is* the positioning, drawn instead of claimed. The hazard-stripe rules, the skewed dashed lane line in `.hero__line` and the road markings behind the amber step discs are coherent instances of one idea: this site is built out of road surfaces.

Then it stops. From the Services grid onward the composition is a standard contractor template: two excellent material-specific feature cards followed by eight interchangeable icon cards; a 2-up comparison; a 4-step numbered timeline; 18 city pills; a split-column accordion; a copy-left/form-right conversion block. Swap the SVG paths and this is a dentist or an HVAC company.

The structural sameness is the problem, not any single section. Seven consecutive full-bleed sections, each ~110px padded, each opening `kicker → h2 → sub`, alternating paper/asphalt/concrete. The rhythm becomes a metronome. On mobile the page is **14,365px tall — 17.7 screens**.

## Overall Impression

This is a well-built page whose distinctive idea is confined to one figure in the hero and abandoned for the next 12,000 pixels. The craft is real — one typeface pair, one accent system, a material vocabulary reused across six components, thorough `prefers-reduced-motion` handling, a genuinely good bilingual implementation. None of that is the problem.

The problem is that the single job of this site is currently broken, and broken in the specific way that is worse than not existing: **the form tells the visitor it succeeded and throws the lead away.** Everything else on this list is a design improvement. That one is a business failure that actively converts warm leads into people who believe they were ignored, in a market whose growth channel is word of mouth.

The biggest opportunity is the one the hero already proves you can execute: stop shaping the page like a site that has photos and reviews, and shape it like a site whose only asset is knowing more about the base than anyone bidding against you.

## What's Working

**1. The pavement cross-section (`.section-cut`).** It works because it is evidence built from CSS at a moment when the business has no evidence. `.layer--base` stacks three offset radial-gradient dot patterns over `#5d574e` and reads unmistakably as compacted road base; the depths turn a sales claim into a spec; the `01/02/03` numbering makes it scannable in two seconds. It answers the one question that separates this contractor from the cheap bid, without a word of self-praise, and no competitor could paste it into their own site.

**2. The material system carried across six components.** Asphalt noise, concrete ruling with control-joint lines, gravel, road-paint amber — reused in `.hero`, `.svc--asphalt`/`.svc--concrete`, `.compare__swatch`, `.steps::before`, `.section-cut::before` and `.footer__stripe`. Every surface is one of the four things this company installs. It also costs zero bytes of imagery, which matters enormously for someone on LTE standing in a driveway.

**3. Spanish treated as a language, not a translation layer.** The pre-paint script sets `lang` before first render, so there is no flash of the wrong language. The register is right — "driveway" and "HOA" stay in English; `quitanieves`, `cajones de estacionamiento`. Plus `hreflang` alternates, `og:locale:alternate`, `knowsLanguage` in the JSON-LD, and translated ARIA strings. More care than most bilingual sites at ten times the budget.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] The quote form tells the visitor it succeeded while destroying the lead

**Why it matters.** This is the site's single job per PRODUCT.md. `script.js` calls `preventDefault()`, checks that name and phone are non-empty, then prints "Thanks! We'll reach out within one business day" and calls `form.reset()`. There is no `action`, no `fetch`, no `mailto` fallback — zero network requests were observed on submit. Every visitor who chooses the form over the phone is silently deleted *and told they were heard*. Those are disproportionately the people least comfortable calling a stranger, which in this market skews Spanish-first. The owner cannot detect the loss because the page reports success. On a site whose entire credibility strategy is "never manufacture proof," the most dishonest string on the page is the success message. Peak-end rule: the visitor's real final memory forms 48 hours later, as "I contacted them and they ignored me."

**Fix.** Until a backend exists, the form must not claim receipt. Either point `<form action>` at Formspree/Netlify Forms — no build step, works on GitHub Pages — and gate the success message on the actual response, with a pending state and a failure branch that surfaces the phone number; or replace the submit handler with an `sms:`/`mailto:` compose built from the collected values and rewrite `form.ok` to describe what actually happened. Not both. Stop calling `form.reset()` until success is confirmed, and add a hidden `lang` field so the crew knows which language to reply in.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable harden`

### [P0] "Recent work / Project gallery" fabricates proof

**Why it matters.** PRODUCT.md names inventing proof as "the failure mode this section exists to prevent." Five CSS-gradient tiles under the kicker "Recent work", each captioned with a real service and a real city — "Stamped Patio — Lehi, UT" — is invented proof; it simply doesn't use sentences. It also fails on its own terms: `.tile--d` and `.tile--e` render as a barcode and a black rectangle, so a three-bid comparison shopper reads either "this site is broken" or "these are fake", at roughly 60% scroll depth — exactly where they are looking for a reason to eliminate one contractor.

**Fix.** Delete the section until real photos exist. In its place put what the business actually has: a "what failure looks like" panel — a cracked and heaved cross-section in the same CSS material language as `.section-cut`, against the correct one. Honest, on-strategy, drawable today, and far more persuasive than five swatches. If the gallery must stay, strip the city names and the word "Recent" and label the tiles explicitly as finish illustrations.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable distill`

### [P1] White on #F15C04 is 3.35:1 on every primary action

**Why it matters.** Measured live: `#fff` on `--orange` = 3.35:1 on the header CTA (16px/700), the hero CTA, the areas CTA and the form submit (18.4px/700 — a quarter-pixel under the 18.66px large-text threshold, so 4.5:1 applies and all fail), the skip link, and the active language button. Hover is worse at 2.68:1. `.svc__num` is 4.17:1, `.layer b` is 3.40:1, and the `.strip` spans fall from 4.17:1 to 2.58:1 across the gradient, failing the large-text 3:1 bar past 69% of its width. PRODUCT.md names this exact case and names the condition: outdoors, bright sun, phone screen. Sunlight does not forgive 3.35:1, and every primary CTA on a conversion-only page sits at the threshold.

**Fix.** Do not abandon the brand orange — darken only the button fill. `#C24700` gives white 4.6:1 and stays unmistakably in the logo's family; keep `#F15C04` for `.dot`, `.strip`, focus rings and the `.areas__list.is-main` chips where it already passes. Raise `.btn` font-size from 1.15rem to 1.2rem so the large-text threshold is cleared with margin rather than missed by a quarter pixel. Pair the form status states with an icon so they are not colour-only.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable audit`

### [P1] Mobile and tablet have no conversion path in the chrome

**Why it matters.** PRODUCT principle 5 is "phone-first: the fastest path from any point in the page is a tap-to-call", and a large share of visitors are on phones standing on the driveway. The chrome contradicts that on every device the strategy names. Three verified holes: at ≤600px both the header phone and the header CTA are `display:none`, and the hamburger menu contains only the five section anchors — no call, no estimate. Between **901px and 1100px** the header phone is hidden by the 1100px query while `.call-fab` is only switched on at ≤900px, so iPad-landscape and 1024×768 laptops have no tap-to-call at all. And the language buttons measure **34.9×28px and 33.8×28px** — the smallest targets on the page, for an interaction PRODUCT.md calls a hard requirement. Two inline `tel:` links are also under target at 119×26 and 94×19.

**Fix.** Add a two-up sticky bottom bar at ≤900px — Call beside Free Estimate — and retire the anonymous FAB, or at minimum move the `.call-fab` breakpoint from 900 to 1100 to close the tablet gap. Put both actions at the top of the mobile menu. Give `.lang__btn` `min-height:44px; min-width:44px`.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable adapt`

### [P2] Validation accepts garbage, and failure gives the user nowhere to go

**Why it matters.** `novalidate` disables native checking and nothing replaces it, so a mistyped phone number is indistinguishable from a real one to the crew — the lead is lost twice over. On failure there is one generic message for either missing field, focus stays on `body`, there is no `aria-invalid` or `aria-describedby`, the only field signal is a red border (colour alone), and the message renders *below* the submit button while the offending fields sit ~600px above it at 375px. An interrupted mobile visitor gets an error they cannot see pointing at a field they cannot find.

**Fix.** Add a phone check (≥10 digits after stripping non-digits) and an email check that runs only when the field is non-empty. Write per-field messages wired with `aria-describedby` and `aria-invalid`, in both languages. Focus the first invalid field on failure. Mark required fields visibly and label Email as optional.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`

## Minor Observations

1. `.strip` is redundant — it lists the same services the grid immediately below lists, is `aria-hidden`, and costs a full screen-width of attention and a 38s infinite animation. (This is the pending `marquee` finding.)
2. Two competing accent families: amber `#e9b949` powers the icon tiles and step discs while orange `#f15c04` is the brand, so on paper backgrounds the eight amber squares read before any service name does.
3. `h1,h2,h3,h4 { line-height: 1 }` is too tight for wrapping card titles — "CURB, GUTTER & ADA RAMPS" collides across two lines.
4. `.form__logo` spends the conversion card's most valuable 62px on a logo that already sits in the fixed header 80px above it. That space should carry the reassurance the section entirely lacks: no obligation, no "we won't sell your number", no "the visit takes about 30 minutes", no hours, no named human.
5. The form has no heading of its own; the `h2` lives in the sibling column, so a screen-reader user tabbing in gets no announcement of what it is.
6. `required` is in the DOM but has no visible marker — AT users hear "required", sighted users see nothing.
7. The ES hero wraps with an orphaned "EL" ending line 2.
8. `scroll-padding-top: 88px` against a 68px mobile header leaves 20px of dead space above every anchor target.
9. `theme-color` `#1a1a1a`, header `rgba(20,20,20,.72)` and `--asphalt` `#141414` are three near-blacks, none matching.
10. The 404 page says "or call us for a free estimate" — and then offers a phone button, but the page has **no heading at all**, so it has no `h1` and no document outline.
11. `config.js` is genuinely good engineering — single source of truth with a localhost-only drift warning. Keep it.
12. `prefers-reduced-motion` handling is thorough and correct, better than most production sites.
13. Landing directly on `#services` leaves 8 of 10 cards at `opacity: 0` until the visitor scrolls one more notch, because they sit right at the observer's threshold boundary.
14. PRODUCT.md is stale on one point: it says "Licensed & insured" appears in the hero, footer **and FAQ**. Verified — it appears only in the hero badge and the footer bar. The FAQ does not mention it.

## Questions to Consider

1. The business has no photos, no reviews and no license paperwork — so why is the page shaped like a page that has them? The gallery, the "Recent work" kicker and the "Licensed & Insured" badge are all empty sockets. What would this page look like if it were designed *for* a company whose only credible asset is knowing more about the base than the competition?
2. The visitor is standing on the driveway holding a camera, and the site asks them to type a description of it. What if the primary mobile CTA were "Text us a photo of your driveway" → `sms:` with a pre-filled body? No backend, one thumb-tap, and better information than any textarea.
3. The page never mentions money, in a category where price is the first question. Is silence the strongest anti-price position — or does it just hand the visitor to the two competitors who *do* publish a range, leaving Geo as the one that "wouldn't tell me"?
4. PRODUCT.md says both audiences must self-identify "within a screen", but an HOA chair scrolls past a driveway hero, a driveway cross-section and eight residential cards. Would a two-door fork — *My home* / *My property or lot* — beat 14,365px of one-size-fits-both?
5. What if the cross-section were not a static figure in the hero but the spine of the whole page — scroll from surface to base to subgrade, each layer revealing the work, the failure it prevents and the Utah-winter consequence? That collapses Services, Compare and Process into one structure no competitor can copy, and cuts the page to a third of its height.
